# Research findings

This repository collects four AI-assisted internal research studies.
Each study folder includes a manuscript and a guide to its evidence,
reproduction steps, and scope. See the [research index](FINDINGS.md)
for an inventory of the publication packets.

## Studies

- [Finite covering certificates for twin-prime candidates](finite-covering/README.md) —
  Gives an exact covering certificate for the band between 37² and 41²,
  with seven twin-prime centers confirmed by a separate sieve
  implementation. The 644-band review checked the consistency of archived
  records.
- [Signed square support on projective rays](projective-ray/readme.md) —
  Proves arithmetic identities under stated hypotheses, including an
  exact fixed-allocation identity, and provides a portable verifier for
  bounded checks. The quasi-Riemann application remains conditional.
  This folder also preserves the original root README.
- [Finite carrier energy bounds](finite-carrier-energy/README.md) —
  Presents a bare-source logarithmic sixth-power bound using stated
  classical inputs. The repeated-decorator fifth-power refinement
  depends on additional stated hypotheses. Exact checks at U = 40
  and U = 80 cover source identities, Gram algebra, signs, and endpoint
  sensitivity.
- [Phase-pair and ellipse predictors for lossless compression](compression-transfer/README.md) —
  Examines complete archive size, exact reconstruction, and transfer,
  with data-dependent gains, neutral controls, and negative results.
  The packet distinguishes the rerun accounting audit from the
  separately recorded replay of 37 retained-artifact decoding checks.

## Scope of the evidence

Exact finite results apply within the stated computational domains.
Retrospective internal checks document what was rerun, replayed, or
checked against archived records. Mathematical proofs apply under their
stated hypotheses, and conditional claims depend on the additional
inputs named in their manuscripts. The computational checks provide
internal evidence, with no claim of independent peer review or a
general production theorem.
